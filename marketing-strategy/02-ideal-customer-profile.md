# 02 · Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) & Where to Find Them

Marketing fails when it targets "everyone in GovCon." Bytescon has a sharp ICP.
Target it relentlessly; ignore the rest until you have traction.

---

## Primary ICP — the bullseye

**GovCon consulting / capture / proposal firms that serve multiple small-business
government contractors.**

- **Size:** 2–50 employees. Sweet spot: 3–15 (big enough to pay, small enough to
  feel the admin pain and decide fast).
- **What they do:** capture management, proposal writing, compliance, and/or
  back-office support for client companies that bid on federal/state contracts.
- **Client book:** 5–30 small-business clients across varied NAICS codes and
  set-aside certifications (SDVOSB, WOSB, HUBZone, 8(a)).
- **Pain:** manual opportunity hunting per client, no consistent go/no-go, compliance
  risk, proposal crunch, and messy post-award finance — all multiplied by client
  count. They can't add clients without adding people.
- **Buying trigger:** onboarding a new client, losing a winnable bid to a missed
  deadline/compliance slip, or a principal deciding to productize/scale the firm.
- **Who signs:** firm owner/principal or ops lead. Short buying chain → fast close.

### Secondary ICP — individual small contractors
Single small businesses that register as their own "client company." Lower ACV,
higher volume, great for self-serve trial funnel and word-of-mouth. Use them to
feed the top of the funnel; they also refer the consultants who serve them.

### Who to de-prioritize (for now)
Large primes and agencies that demand FedRAMP/CMMC today — the product honestly
isn't there yet. Log them; don't chase them.

## Firmographic & segment targeting

Highest-intent sub-segments to name and pursue first:

1. **Veteran-owned consulting firms** — there's a built-in 10% veteran discount;
   lean into veteran business networks (NVSBC, SDVOSB communities).
2. **8(a) / HUBZone / WOSB-focused consultancies** — set-aside intelligence is a
   direct value match.
3. **Firms clustered around procurement hubs** — DC/Maryland/Virginia (the "DMV"),
   plus San Antonio, Huntsville, Colorado Springs, Tampa.
4. **State & local-focused consultancies** — the State & Municipal add-on + scrapers
   for NY, TX, FL, VA, GA, NC, OH, IL, CA, MD, PA map to where these firms operate.

## Where they gather (go here)

**Online communities & media**
- LinkedIn (the #1 channel for GovCon — see 03) — groups, hashtags (#GovCon,
  #FederalContracting, #SmallBusiness), and individual thought leaders.
- Reddit: r/govcon, r/fednews-adjacent threads.
- GovCon podcasts & newsletters (e.g., GovCon Chamber, DoD Contract Academy,
  Govology, GovCon Giants) — guest spots and sponsorships.
- Facebook/LinkedIn groups for 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB owners.

**Organizations & events**
- **APEX Accelerators** (formerly PTACs) — government-funded centers that counsel
  small businesses; partner with them (see 03 — partnerships).
- **SBA** district offices and SBDCs; SCORE chapters.
- **NCMA** (National Contract Management Association), **AFCEA**, **NDIA**,
  **PSC** (Professional Services Council), **WIPP**, **NVSBC**.
- Regional small-business matchmaking events, industry days, GovCon Summit,
  DoD/agency small-business conferences.

**Discovery lists (how to build a named-account list)**
- **SAM.gov entity search** — filter registered entities by NAICS tied to
  consulting/professional services and by set-aside status; these are prospects
  and the clients your prospects serve.
- **USAspending / FPDS** — find firms repeatedly listed as proposal/consulting
  support; find small-biz winners who likely use a consultant.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — title filters: "capture manager," "proposal
  manager," "president" at firms with "GovCon/federal consulting" in the
  description; company size 2–50.
- **APEX Accelerator client directories** and SBA resource partner lists.

> Dogfood it: Bytescon's own discovery + recipient-lookup tools can surface the
> firms and small businesses that make the best prospects. Use the product to
> build the pipeline — and it becomes a demo story.

## Buyer vs. user map (who to speak to in the message)

| Role | Cares about | Reach via |
|------|------------|-----------|
| **Principal / owner (buyer)** | Margin, scaling clients, firm brand | LinkedIn, referrals, podcasts |
| **Capture / BD lead (champion)** | Win rate, fewer dead-end pursuits | LinkedIn, demos, webinars |
| **Proposal manager (user)** | Compliance, speed | Content (compliance guides), demos |
| **Finance/contracts (user)** | DCAA readiness, invoicing | Case studies, back-office content |

## ICP qualification checklist (use on every lead)

A lead is **well-qualified** if ≥4 are true:
- [ ] Serves 3+ small-business clients (or is an ambitious 1–2 client firm scaling)
- [ ] Bids on federal and/or state contracts
- [ ] Feels pain in opportunity hunting, compliance, or proposal volume
- [ ] 2–50 employees
- [ ] Owner/ops lead is reachable (short buying chain)
- [ ] Not blocked by a hard FedRAMP/CMMC-today requirement
